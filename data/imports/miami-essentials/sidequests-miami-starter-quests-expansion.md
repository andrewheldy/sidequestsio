SideQuests — Miami Starter Quest Expansion

Prepared: October 23, 2026
Status: Proposed content and product recommendations. Not published, not field-tested, and not implemented in the application.
Relationship to the original: Companion to “sidequests — Miami Staples Starter Quests.md.” The original file is unchanged. Numbering continues from its 20 quests.

Recommendation

Keep the original collection, but present it as Miami Essentials, with a smaller Start Here subset. The strongest first experience is a nearby, understandable action with a small commitment and a clear finish—not a boat rental, a cross-city expedition, or an all-night commitment.

The expansion below adds 20 concepts: 15 named-location quests, two food templates requiring venue selection, and three reusable formats requiring a concrete location. All are independent editorial proposals; none imply a venue partnership or endorsement.

These durations are design estimates for the activity itself, not travel time, queues, guaranteed completion time, or full attraction visits. “Free” describes the proposed activity/admission where stated; parking and transportation can still cost money. Official pages were consulted for the facts cited below, but live hours, access, tickets, weather, and local rules must be checked before publication. Exact geofences and accessibility claims require a site check.

Product rules for the proposed catalog

Start Here: Curate a compact first screen of quick, nearby, low-cost quests. Keep difficulty, cost, schedule and partnership status as separate attributes.

Evidence: Treat photo + foreground GPS as supporting evidence, not proof of all described behavior. Check-in or self-reported tasks should be labeled accordingly. Do not claim these modes already exist: the uploaded draft itself flags a mismatch with the QR-first MVP. Keep QR flows for authorized partner/event checkpoints and add a distinct self-guided completion path only after inspecting the actual application.

Permissions and privacy: Follow venue photography and access rules. User posts should be private by default unless the user chooses otherwise; social sharing, public reviews, partner tags and marketing consent should not be conditions for ordinary exploration. Do not assume a personal visitor photo is licensed for sponsor promotion. Keep location collection limited to the completion need and provide an appropriate manual fallback.

Rewards: The draft’s Easy = 50 XP and Medium = 150 XP are retained here only as proposed XP labels. Redeemable points are not newly promised or budgeted by this document. Set funding, limits, anti-duplication rules and eligibility before attaching monetary-value rewards to freely repeatable public-place tasks. Paid admission or greater risk should not automatically mean higher rewards.

Small launch, broader backlog: First publish a field-checked, geographically coherent selection, then release more as the completion flow and content are tested. A useful nearby set is more important than an inflated total pin count.

21. Through the Fly’s Eye

Where: Design District — Fly’s Eye Dome, Palm Court
Shelf/type: Quick start
Proposed difficulty: Easy · 50 XP
Estimated activity time: 10–15 minutes
Cost/access: No admission purchase for the proposed public-viewing-area task; transport/parking separate

Objective: Find the dome and photograph it from an angle that makes it look like it landed from another planet.

Completion evidence: One in-app photo from a permitted viewing area plus a foreground location check. This supports presence and the pictured object, not an entire visit.

Before publishing: Stay in publicly accessible viewing areas; do not enter the installation or water feature for a shot.

Initial new-quest shortlist

Prioritize Through the Fly’s Eye, Garage or Gallery?, Under the City, Miami Before Miami, Overtown Encore, Garden Reset, Española Way Postcard, and Art Critic for a Minute after resolving any access or gallery limitations. Add a selected food venue only after checking its menu, hours and photo expectations.

Keep paid gardens/museums, scheduled concerts, buddy variants, and more distant outdoor outings available in their own shelves rather than making them prerequisites to onboarding.

How this fits the activation strategy

The proposed role for these independent quests is the always-available consumer experience: something useful before, between, and after paid activations. Treat retention and sponsor value as hypotheses to test, not results already established.

Separately, authorized event or partner quests can carry funded rewards, approved campaign actions and reporting. Do not put a sponsor logo, permission claim, marketing checkbox, or lead-transfer promise onto an independent venue listing by default. Keep participation and marketing consent separate.
